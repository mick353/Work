# Branching decision practice

## The idea in plain English

This is an optional future upgrade for a course. Instead of answering one
multiple-choice question and moving on, a learner works through a short,
realistic situation and sees what their choices lead to.

For example, a stakeholder asks for an urgent new feature. The learner chooses
whether to add it immediately, investigate the underlying need, or pause and
align the people involved. Their choice changes what happens next. They then
make a follow-up decision and receive an explanation of the trade-off.

The point is not to catch someone out. It is to practise judgement in a safe
way: weighing evidence, risks, stakeholders and likely consequences.

## Why it could be useful

Some parts of training are about knowing information. Others are about making
sound decisions when the answer depends on context. Branching practice is for
the second kind.

It could help learners:

- connect a principle to a realistic work situation;
- see why a tempting answer may create a problem later; and
- compare different reasonable approaches before applying one at work.

## What a trainer would create

If we decide to build this into Course Workshop, a trainer would use a simple
form to add:

1. the starting situation;
2. the choices a learner can make;
3. what happens after each choice; and
4. the explanation or coaching message for that point.

No coding should be required. A finished scenario would end with a short
summary of the reasoning, rather than simply declaring a learner right or
wrong.

## What it would not replace

This would supplement—not replace—the existing lesson, knowledge checks,
worked examples, source material and capstone. It is best used sparingly for
decisions that genuinely involve competing priorities or consequences.

## Current position

This is a proposed enhancement, not a feature currently enabled in Course
Workshop. The sensible next step is to hear whether trainers see useful,
real-world decisions in their courses that would benefit from this format.
