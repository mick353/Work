# Product Practice --- Commercialisation and Scale Plan

**Status:** Commercial strategy and execution plan\
**Version:** 1.0\
**Date:** 20 September 2026

## Executive decision

Product Practice should initially be commercialised as an
**organisational authoring licence + paid enablement pilot + optional
quality/release support**, not primarily as an online course, LMS, SaaS
platform or bespoke instructional-design consultancy.

The customer buys the capability for its trainers to convert existing
workplace knowledge---policies, procedures, standards, manuals,
presentations and subject-matter expertise---into structured interactive
training.

``` text
CURRENT TRAINER PILOT
→ prove independent trainer use
→ clean commercial demonstration
→ PAID ORGANISATIONAL PILOT
→ one real customer course
→ ANNUAL TEAM / ORGANISATION LICENCE
→ more trainers + courses
→ optional QA / enablement / support
→ larger enterprise / agency licences
→ commercial-producer / partner licences
```

The immediate commercial test is simple: **can an organisation outside
the original workplace use Product Practice to turn its own material
into a useful course with limited assistance, and will it pay to retain
that capability?**

## 1. What is being sold

The repository's core model is: **a course is data; the player is
code.** Course Workshop is the trainer-facing authoring application; the
exported learner course is separate from authoring. Current architecture
is local/offline-capable, requires no learner account or application
server for authoring, supports individual course export, keeps current
learner progress/drafts local unless deliberately transferred, and
preserves a controlled release role.

Commercially, this is best framed as:

> **A governed workplace-knowledge-to-training production system.**

The existing courses are demonstrations and proof. The scalable assets
are the Workshop, learner player, authoring/release method and package
contract.

## 2. Customer problem

Organisations often already possess the knowledge they need to teach.
The unresolved problem is conversion:

``` text
POLICY / PROCEDURE / MANUAL / DECK / SME KNOWLEDGE
→ instructional structure
→ practice
→ assessment
→ feedback
→ workplace application
→ source traceability
→ review
→ DEPLOYABLE TRAINING
```

Product Practice should be positioned around this conversion job.

## 3. What not to do first

Do not lead with selling the existing courses. Do not rebuild Product
Practice as an LMS or SaaS product before paid demand proves that need.
Do not compete feature-for-feature with Articulate. Do not become a
bespoke development shop with a separate codebase for every customer. Do
not add AI merely because competitors advertise AI.

Every major addition should either remove a measured adoption blocker,
reduce support burden, or increase independent trainer success.

## 4. Primary business model: land and expand

### Free evaluation

Use a public demonstration, short demo video, example learner course and
controlled Workshop evaluation to create qualified pilot conversations.
The objective is not self-service consumer checkout.

### Paid organisational pilot

Offer:

> **Bring one real training need and its source material. Your trainer
> will use Product Practice to produce one deployable training
> package.**

Suggested contents: - 60--90 day evaluation licence - up to 2--5
nominated trainers - onboarding - one real customer course - one or two
review checkpoints - learner export - basic deployment assistance -
limited email support - end-of-pilot review

**Indicative test price: AUD \$4,000--\$8,000 + GST.** A practical first
hypothesis is **\$5,900 + GST**. This is a pricing experiment, not
established willingness-to-pay.

Exclude unlimited consulting, custom development, guaranteed LMS
integration, 24/7 support and responsibility for the customer's source
accuracy/approval.

## 6. Commercial-producer licence

Separate internal-use rights from rights to use Product Practice to
produce training commercially for clients. Training consultancies can
become distribution multipliers. Price commercial-production rights
above ordinary internal use.

This channel should come after you can reliably onboard and support an
ordinary organisation.

## 7. Services

Use services to accelerate licence adoption rather than replace
licensing: - trainer onboarding - first-course enablement - course
quality/release review - premium first-course conversion -
deployment/branding assistance - premium support

Avoid a permanent model where revenue equals your personal delivery
hours.

## 8. Recurring revenue

A clean model is **annual authoring licence + maintained versions +
updates + support**.

The eventual commercial agreement can distinguish continuing use of
previously exported learner courses from continuing rights to author new
courses, receive updates and obtain support. Have a commercial/IP lawyer
draft the actual terms.

## 9. Current trainer pilot

The trainers now trying Course Workshop are the highest-value commercial
experiment currently available.

Capture: - prior authoring experience - real task/source used - time to
first useful preview - time to usable course - assistance required -
points of confusion - suggestions - output produced - whether they would
build another course - whether they would recommend it

The key measure is **independent transfer**: can another trainer succeed
without the creator driving the process?

Also track **support-to-output ratio**: how much of your time is
required for a customer to produce one useful course? This is a crucial
scalability indicator.

## 10. Commercial clean-room boundary

Before external sale, obtain professional advice on ownership and
employment/IP boundaries. The repository NOTICE distinguishes
DEWR/source artefacts from original learning/application work, and the
repository licence asserts proprietary rights over original material,
but that does not by itself settle every employment/IP question.

Create an external edition containing: - Product Practice player -
Course Workshop - generic branding - generic documentation/templates -
one demonstration course you clearly own or have permission to
commercialise - no DEWR branding or restricted/internal material - no
implication of departmental endorsement

This is a gating action.

## 11. Demonstration strategy

Build one short, excellent, legally clean demonstration rather than
another large course. Its purpose is to show the transformation:

``` text
SOURCE MATERIAL
→ COURSE STRUCTURE
→ PRACTICE
→ ASSESSMENT
→ APPLICATION
→ REVIEW
→ LEARNER PACKAGE
```

A 20--40 minute demonstration course is sufficient.

## 12. Marketing

Initially use demonstration-led B2B selling, not paid advertising.

Core message:

> **Bring one procedure, policy, manual, deck or training requirement.
> See how your trainer can turn it into a complete learning package.**

Suggested website headline:

> **Turn workplace knowledge into training people can actually use.**

Supporting line:

> Product Practice helps workplace trainers convert policies,
> procedures, manuals and source material into structured interactive
> courses---without coding.

Calls to action: - See an example - Try Course Workshop - Run a pilot

## 13. Three-minute demo

1.  Show a real source document and the training problem.
2.  Open Course Workshop and show source-to-stage structure.
3.  Show teaching, assessment, scenario, feedback and applied work.
4.  Show review/checks and explain human approval remains authoritative.
5.  Export/open the learner package.
6.  Close with: **"Give us one real training problem. We will pilot it
    with your trainer."**

## 14. Buyers

Likely champions/buyers include: - L&D managers - capability managers -
internal training teams - operational excellence teams - policy
implementation teams - compliance/onboarding owners - quality/assurance
teams - professional-services training producers

Distinguish the trainer/end user, economic buyer, subject-matter
approver, IT/security reviewer and procurement officer.

## 15. Qualification

Strong early customers have real source material, recurring training
demand, a trainer/SME, an immediate course need and authority to run a
modest pilot.

Avoid early customers demanding a full LMS, SSO, complex analytics,
bespoke integrations, 24/7 support and extensive custom development
before they have tested the core product.

## 16. Sales discovery questions

Ask: 1. What training are you trying to produce? 2. What source governs
it? 3. Who converts it into training today? 4. How long does that take?
5. What is difficult about the current process? 6. How many similar
training needs exist? 7. How often does the source change? 8. Who
reviews/approves training? 9. How is it distributed? 10. Is central
completion tracking mandatory? 11. What would make a pilot impossible?
12. Who approves a pilot? 13. If it works, where else would you use it?

The most valuable expansion question is: **"How many other things do you
need to turn into training?"**

## 17. Government path

Do not begin with a whole-of-government sale.

Use:

``` text
current internal proof
→ another team/business area
→ clean external pilot
→ one agency
→ multiple teams
→ other agencies
→ cross-agency evidence
→ larger licence/procurement
```

As at September 2026, the Commonwealth non-construction procurement
threshold for non-corporate Commonwealth entities is \$125,000. Division
1 applies to all procurements; additional rules generally apply at/above
the relevant threshold. Current rules also contain
Australian-business/SME mechanisms, and Finance guidance describes an
SME exemption allowing direct engagement up to \$500,000 including GST
where its conditions are satisfied and value for money can be
demonstrated.

This does **not** mean a buyer can automatically award any sub-threshold
contract. The practical point is that a modest paid pilot/business-unit
licence can be a realistic procurement unit; you do not need a
whole-of-government tender to start.

Eventually prepare: - ABN/business entity and GST arrangements as
applicable - business bank account/domain/email - capability statement -
licence/services agreements - privacy and data-handling statement -
security architecture summary - accessibility evidence - insurance as
required - supplier onboarding information

Monitor AusTender and relevant arrangements, but do not make tender
chasing the initial sales strategy.

## 18. Corporate path

Use the same pilot model. Good sectors are those with changing
procedures, regulated work, small training teams, expensive outsourced
design or large backlogs of source material.

Potential sectors include professional services, utilities,
construction, finance/insurance, healthcare administration,
not-for-profits and training consultancies.

Start where you have warm credibility rather than marketing to every
sector.

## 19. Competitive positioning

**Versus documents/PowerPoint:** adds structured learning, practice,
assessment, application and traceability.

**Versus outsourced instructional design:** aims to make internal
trainers more self-sufficient and reusable.

**Versus broad authoring suites:** narrower, source-led and
simpler/local in its current architecture.

**Versus LMSs:** authoring/learner-package system, not initially learner
management.

**Versus AI course generation:** governed source authority, human review
and explicit release boundaries.

Articulate's current published pricing is roughly USD \$1,449--\$1,749
per author/year for its main L&D authoring offering and includes a far
broader enterprise feature set. Do not position Product Practice as
"cheap Articulate"; position it around the specific source-to-training
job.

## 20. Support model

**Standard licence:** documentation, updates and bounded email support.

**Paid enablement:** onboarding, build workshop, first-course
assistance.

**Premium:** priority response, scheduled reviews, QA and deployment
help.

Do not promise 24/7 support, unlimited calls, unlimited review or
unlimited custom development.

Documentation should include a Quick Start, Trainer Guide, Reviewer
Guide, Release Guide, IT/Security Overview, Known Limitations, FAQ and
Support Policy.

## 21. Security/privacy

Describe the architecture precisely rather than claiming generic
security. Current useful facts include local drafts/progress, no
required application account/server for current authoring and no current
application telemetry. Local files can still contain sensitive
information.

For serious buyers, prepare a data-flow and dependency document covering
storage, network calls, update/release process, browser requirements,
vulnerability handling and support lifecycle.

## 22. Accessibility

The repository correctly recognises that automated checks do not prove
accessibility conformance. Before making formal accessibility claims,
perform manual keyboard, screen-reader, zoom/reflow, colour/contrast and
device testing, and obtain specialist assessment where procurement
requires it.

## 23. AI

AI should be an accelerator only when it removes a measured authoring
bottleneck. Possible later uses include draft stages, questions,
scenarios, glossary extraction and source mapping. Preserve source
boundaries, review, traceability and human release authority.

Do not turn the product into a generic "AI course generator".

## 24. Feature-request rule

Classify requests: - **Blocks multiple credible paid sales:** strong
candidate. - **Repeated by serious prospects:** roadmap candidate. -
**Helpful preference:** defer. - **Turns Product Practice into another
product:** usually reject.

## 25. Pricing mechanics

Do not price from development hours alone. Test against customer value,
alternatives, support burden, organisational scope and expected course
volume.

Ask: **"How many training packages will you need over the next 12
months, and how are you producing them today?"**

Use pilots to create real ROI evidence. Do not promise unmeasured
savings.

## 26. Twelve-month sequence

### Phase 1 --- Prove transfer (now--6 weeks)

Complete current trainer pilot, capture evidence, fix genuine blockers,
measure support required.

**Gate:** another trainer can produce meaningful work without you
driving the process.

### Phase 2 --- Clear commercial boundary (parallel, 2--8 weeks)

Professional IP/employment review; separate departmental/source content;
generic branding; clean demonstration.

**Gate:** external edition contains only material that can lawfully be
commercialised.

### Phase 3 --- Package the offer (weeks 6--10)

Landing page, 3-minute demo, pilot offer, capability statement, pricing
hypothesis, FAQ, data/security summary and commercial documents.

### Phase 4 --- First paid pilots (months 3--6)

Target 3--5 organisations. Goal: **1--3 paid pilots**, not thousands of
leads.

### Phase 5 --- Licence conversion (months 4--9)

Convert successful pilots to annual licences. Refine onboarding, support
and pricing.

### Phase 6 --- Repeatable selling (months 6--12)

Case studies, referrals, repeatable proposals, government/corporate
procurement pack and first commercial-producer test.

### Phase 7 --- Scale decision

Only after evidence choose among a profitable licensing business,
enterprise product, government specialist product, partner network, SaaS
or licensing/acquisition by a larger vendor.

## 29. Pilot close

At pilot end ask: - Do you want to keep using it? - Who else would
author? - How many courses next year? - Which teams? - What support is
needed? - What prevents an annual licence? - Who approves that purchase?

Then issue the annual proposal.

## 30. Objections

**"We already have an LMS."**\
Product Practice is not initially replacing it. Establish whether the
authoring/export model fits their environment.

**"We already have Articulate."**\
Articulate is broad. Product Practice is a narrower source-led workplace
authoring/review workflow. Test whether that workflow solves a gap.

**"We need analytics."**\
The current local player does not provide central learner analytics.
Establish whether that is mandatory before sale.

**"Can AI build it automatically?"**\
AI may accelerate drafting later; source authority, review and release
remain human-governed.

**"Can you customise it?"**\
Test the standard product first; distinguish configuration from bespoke
product development.

## 31. Founder decision rules

Build when a feature blocks multiple credible paid opportunities,
reduces support burden, increases independent trainer success or
strengthens the core transformation.

Sell services when they accelerate adoption and licensing. Avoid
services that make you the permanent author.

## 32. RDM commercial interpretation

The commercial resolution chain is:

``` text
ORGANISATION HAS KNOWLEDGE
→ needs workforce capability
→ knowledge must become training
→ trainer needs production method/tool
→ PRODUCT PRACTICE
→ course produced
→ human review/approval
→ learner package deployed
→ organisation sees outcome
→ additional training backlog becomes visible
→ repeat use
→ licence value
```

The highest-leverage commercial node is **successful independent trainer
production of a real organisational course**.

That event can generate customer value, product proof, trainer adoption,
management confidence, further course demand, licence demand, case
evidence and referrals simultaneously.

## 33. Minimum-effort monetisation architecture

``` text
EXISTING PRODUCT
→ CURRENT TRAINER PILOT
→ CLEAN DEMO
→ ~$5.9K PAID PILOT
→ $6K–$25K TEAM/BUSINESS-UNIT ANNUAL LICENCE
→ OPTIONAL ENABLEMENT / QA
→ LARGER ORGANISATIONAL LICENCE
→ COMMERCIAL-PRODUCER LICENCE
```

Do not insert SaaS, LMS, AI, a marketplace, learner subscriptions,
advertising or a large sales operation between the trainer pilot and the
first paid pilot.

## 34. Illustrative small-business economics

This is not a forecast, only a demonstration that organisational
licensing does not require consumer scale:

``` text
5 team licences × $8,000             $40,000
5 business-unit licences × $18,000   $90,000
3 pilots × $5,900                    $17,700
4 enablement engagements × $3,000    $12,000
                                     -------
Illustrative annual revenue         $159,700
```

A few larger licences could produce a different mix with fewer
customers.

## 35. Stop conditions

Stop or reposition if evidence shows trainers cannot independently use
the system, support burden remains extreme, legal/IP boundaries prevent
commercialisation, credible buyers will not pay, or required customer
capabilities fundamentally conflict with the architecture.

Negative evidence is useful. Do not protect the idea from the market.

## 36. Immediate decision

The project is at:

``` text
CREATOR SUCCESS
→ OTHER TRAINER SUCCESS
→ EXTERNAL PAID SUCCESS
```

The current trainer trial is testing the second transition.

The next major product asset is not another feature. It is **evidence
that another trainer can create a real course successfully**.

The next commercial experiment after that is a **paid external
organisational pilot**.

Everything else should support those two outcomes.

# Appendix C --- Claims discipline

Current repository evidence supports describing Product Practice as a
trainer-facing authoring tool capable of producing individual learner
packages; local/offline-capable; based on a versioned training-package
model; designed to separate source material from the learning platform;
structured around source authority and human review; and supported by
bounded automated checks.

Do not yet claim proven cost savings, superior learning outcomes, formal
WCAG conformance, enterprise security certification, LMS compatibility,
whole-of-government approval, universal regulatory compliance, proven
customer ROI or scalable commercial adoption without additional
evidence.

# Appendix E --- Operating principle

> **Do not build the company that a hypothetical future customer might
> need. Sell the useful product that already exists, observe where real
> paid adoption fails, and spend effort only where removing that failure
> increases repeatable organisational use.**

For Product Practice:

> **trainer proof → paid pilot → annual licence → organisational
> expansion → partner distribution.**
